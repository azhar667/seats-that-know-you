01 / The idea

One cabin. Different people.
Personal experiences.

Cars are shared by many, but personalization is usually designed around one person. We connect each passenger with the seat they occupy — and the moment they are in.

01 — RECOGNITION

Who sits where

An existing interior camera identifies known profiles and maps each person to a seat. Unknown passengers are welcomed as guests.

02 — MY SEAT

A personal zone

Each seat can respond through its own light identity, screen experience, sound zone and available comfort settings.

03 — MOVES WITH YOU

The experience follows

When a person changes seats, their experience follows them. Profiles are designed to travel across compatible vehicles; guest data is not saved.

Person+Seat+Moment→Contextual response
02 / The design tension

Shared cabin.
Unequal control.

Manual control asks every passenger to manage too much. Whole-car control changes too much. The opportunity is to change only the zone that needs it.

12×Children in the back were reported as more distracting to drivers than a phone call in the cited Monash research.
69%DTS (2022): respondents who considered contextual personalisation important.
92%Blue Yonder (2024): respondents who said they would pay extra for personalisation.
THE CURRENT GAP

Cars remember seat settings — not always the person.

Memory-seat buttons preserve a position in a seat slot. When people swap places, the next passenger may inherit someone else’s settings. Existing biometric systems tend to focus on the driver; shared rear-seat experiences remain fragmented.

THE DESIGN OPPORTUNITY

Link “who” with “where”.

Let the car understand who is present and where they are sitting, then make each person’s experience visible without disturbing everyone else.

What the precedent map showed

Genesis Face Connect personalises the driver; Mercedes ambient lighting communicates cabin states; NIO’s assistant responds at a broader vehicle level. The team identified an opportunity between driver-only personalisation and whole-cabin responses: a visible experience for every passenger.

What the research did not prove

Desk research informed the direction, but the archive explicitly noted that user interviews were still needed. Figures are retained with their cited source labels and should be checked against original publications before external use.

03 / One ride, many needs

Personalisation is contextual.

The concept began with a family scenario, then expanded beyond a child-centred story to a system intended for every passenger.

Sofia
PRIMARY NARRATIVE · FAMILY RIDE

Sofia, 36 — a parent and driver

On a 16-minute ride home, Sofia cannot easily see what is happening in the rear seats without turning around. Leo (6) sits rear-right; Mia (2) sits in a rear-facing child seat on the left.

Problem: looking back takes attention from the road
Intervention: seat states become visible
Change: one glance can be enough
SCENARIO A · SLEEPING LIGHT

A quiet signal instead of an announcement

The camera detects that Mia is asleep. Her seat’s light fades to a very low warm level (around 3%), visible to Sofia in the mirror. Leo’s media remains in his own zone; the Cabin Map summarises the cabin state. The design avoids a spoken “Mia is asleep” alert.

SCENARIO B · GUEST COMFORT

Margaret joins the ride

Margaret (72) is a guest who may feel cold and may not know the menus. The concept uses a warm visual cue and one simple question, rather than asking her to navigate a complex interface or ask the driver.

Important scope decision: Sofia’s family ride is the narrative entry point, not the limit of the product. The wider system also considers friends, guests, passengers who change seats, and people who need different experiences at the same time.

06 / System & interaction

One system, from arrival to exit.

The car is the primary experience. The app supports first-time setup, profile management and post-ride summaries.

Camera senses→Profile or guest→Seat becomes a zone→Light · screen · sound · comfort
ARRIVE

Welcome

A known passenger sees their name and personal colour. An unknown passenger receives a neutral Champagne welcome; their face is not saved.

DURING THE RIDE

My Seat

Personal home, media, individual sound, temperature and lighting controls. A passenger’s experience can move with them when seats change.

DRIVER VIEW

Cabin Map

A compact overview shows who is sitting where and relevant states such as sleep, sharing or warmth, reducing the need to turn around.

Seat and camera states

Empty · Recognised · Guest · Active · Sleeping · Sharing. Recognition should be stable across several frames before the displayed identity changes. If uncertain, the system falls back to Welcome / Guest rather than assigning the wrong name.

Interaction ideas explored

Sleep / wake detection, gaze-away pause, hand gesture acceptance, screen-sharing, sound-edge warning, warmth prompts, motion cues before turns or stops, and reminders for people or objects left behind. Not all are equally mature or validated.

App flow

One-time setup: account → personal profile and colour → add family members with consent → connect a vehicle through Digital Key. Later visits open the home screen with family, vehicle status, recent ride and notifications.

Screen structure

15 screen concepts were grouped into arrival, in-ride passenger screens and driver screens: Welcome, Guest QR, personal home, media, sharing, conflict resolution, settings, protected zone, Goodbye, Cabin Map, quiet alerts and exit reminders.

Conflict and permission logic

Saved personal choices come first. Then the system checks, in order: safety; whether a vulnerable passenger needs protection; whether one seat zone can solve the issue alone; and whether two equally prioritised passengers need to be asked. The guiding rule is: if the system is confident, it acts; if it is not, it asks.

07 / Visual language

Calm, warm and readable.

The final direction connects the vehicle interface to Hyundai ccNC / Seon line language and the IONIQ 6 mood-light colour family, without copying the existing palette.

COLOUR SYSTEM

One person, one colour.

Champagne is the neutral / guest state. Eight seat colours distinguish passenger identities. Colour is reinforced by seat position, names and motion so it is never the only way to understand a state.

LIGHT LANGUAGE

Four indirect zones.

Door lines · screen halo · console lines · under-seat glow. No ceiling spots, no roof strip, no light near the windshield or mirrors. Light communicates recognition, sleep, sound, sharing, motion, warmth and seat changes.

Timing reference: resting level 15%; peaks 35–55%; gentle fades; no flashing. Driver zone remains dim warm white.

Typography & layout

Pretendard for English and Korean. Vehicle text scale: 48 / 36 / 28 / 24 / 18 px; avoid sizes below 18 px. App reference: 390 × 844 px, 14 px body, 12 px secondary text, 28–31 px headings.

Display references

Rear display: 11.6 in, 16:10, 1280 × 800. Centre and front passenger reference: 12.3 in, 1920 × 720. Hyundai’s Seon line motif informs the thin personal-colour line at the top of each seat screen.

Nine documented light states

01 · Welcome

Personal colour travels door line → under-seat glow → screen halo; brief peak, then a low resting state.

02 · Guest

Same sequence in a neutral / Champagne tone; no profile is stored.

03 · Sleeping

All zones of that seat fade from 15% to about 3% over 10 seconds, then remain dim. The detailed v2 light spec specifies a slow fade, not flashing.

04 · Sound edge

A soft edge shimmer warns that sound is reaching a neighbouring zone; sound may then be reduced only on that side.

05 · Share

The sender’s colour travels from their screen halo across the console line to the receiving screen.

06 · Motion cue

A light cue moves toward the side of an upcoming turn.

07 · Warmth

Light shifts from a cooler tone toward a warmer tone when heating is selected.

08 · Child reminder

Amber (#E39B2F) signals a child reminder when the vehicle is parked; red is not used.

09 · Seat change

The passenger’s colour hops from the old seat to the new one, with a documented 0.9-second transition.

08 / Prototype & privacy

Evidence before promises.

The prototype tested whether a camera could identify enrolled people and associate them with left / right seat screens in a controlled setup.

EARLIER CAMERA TEST

8 / 9 correct attempts

The documented test reported eight correct recognitions in nine attempts. In the unsuccessful case, the system showed Guest rather than a wrong person’s name.

PROTOTYPE V2

Two-seat mapping

Known faces are mapped to the corresponding left or right display. The prototype also explored sleep / wake detection and seat changes.

KNOWN LIMITS

Controlled conditions

Recognition weakened in low light and with heavy makeup. The archive records the need for multi-person testing, stable matching and a safe fallback.

PRIVACY PRINCIPLES

Local processing, no guest profile.

The concept specifies in-vehicle processing, no stored video, no saved guest identity, a camera-off option and consent / deletion controls. For babies, the concept is to recognise the child seat rather than claim facial recognition, with parent confirmation.

TEST GATE

Accuracy is not enough.

The original continuation rule was at least 9 correct results out of 10 in a bright room, with no incorrect name ever shown to an unknown person. User testing was still listed as not yet completed in the 10 November archive.

01 / Observe & frame

Start with the people inside the vehicle.

The semester began with field observation at Hyundai UX Studio Seoul on 7 September 2026. The team explored the UX Test Zone, SDV Zone and UX Archive Zone. Sliding doors, movable seats and consoles, Gleo AI voice control, and an exhibition about the experience of vision raised a broader question: what happens when people share a vehicle but need different things at the same time?

Field observations

The team saw sliding-door demonstrations, physical vehicle mock-ups, a voice assistant that could open the trunk and fuel door, and research prompts about colour-vision accessibility, AR HUDs, visual fatigue, pixel light, eye tracking and night adaptation.

Frame 1 · 14 September

Three case studies explored mobility and travel: Hyundai sliding doors / Zoox, BMW Theatre Screen, and NIO EVE / Renault SYMBIOZ. The shared thread was that passengers in the same car can want different things at the same moment.

What the first critique taught

The presentation was too descriptive: it listed facts but did not connect them into one design question. The team adopted a stronger sequence: Observe → question → research and case → interpretation → one design principle.

Guiding principle

“The vehicle adapts to each person — instead of each person adapting to the vehicle.” Context is relational: it depends on the person, their seat and the moment, not a fixed seat preset alone.

02 / Imagine · 28 September–5 October

Three answers to one mobility question.

The shared research question asked how a vehicle could understand each passenger as an individual: who they are, how their body experiences movement, and how they want to be with others. The team explored three complementary directions before selecting one.

DIRECTION 01 · SELECTED

Passenger-Centered Personalization

A vehicle recognises who sits in each seat and adapts the experience to person + seat + moment. Memory seats remember a slot, not the person; existing biometric systems largely serve the driver.

DIRECTION 02 · EXTENSION

Adaptive Motion Comfort

From road-aware to human-aware mobility. Sense movement and passenger state, predict discomfort, prepare with cues and adapt. The research cited anticipatory vibration cues; visual cues still needed their own evidence.

DIRECTION 03 · NOT SELECTED

Modular Social Pods

Four linked single-seat capsules with Together, Split and Close Off modes. The idea explored the space between travelling together and having personal space, but introduced a much larger physical and safety scope.

Direction 01 was selected on 10 October. Motion Comfort remained a possible future service, not part of the core storyboard. Modular Social Pods was not pursued as the main project.

04 / Critique log

Feedback became design constraints.

Professor feedback repeatedly asked the team to make the project more concrete, visually communicative and feasible. The team recorded not only what changed, but why.

14 September · Frame 1

There was no central question; the three sections felt like separate mini-presentations. The response was to connect field observation, theory and precedent through one design principle.

9 October · Imagine 2 feedback

Fingerprint recognition was considered expensive and unnecessary. Use existing camera sensors and an interface layer; choose one direction and show the visual, video and spatial experience of several personas in one situation.

12 October · Direction 01

The concept and research approach were positively received. The next iteration needed camera placement, multiple scenarios, vehicle-scale thinking, and more precise light, sound and screen designs.

19 October · Scope

Define exactly what would be made in each integrated course. Treat the 3D Cabin Map as a lower priority; use the Hyundai UX Studio Figma template as a reference.

26 October · Profiles across vehicles

For irregular users, consider an account that can work across multiple vehicles rather than data stored in one car only.

2 November · Prototype 1

Align screens with Hyundai’s interface system; test more than one person and combinations; investigate what the camera can do beyond Welcome; consider colour accessibility and whether colour is necessary. A connected app was also requested.

8–9 November · Narrative and scope

Use one persona and one concrete problem, following Concept → Situation → Experience → Problem → Design Intervention → Change. The family story is an entry point, not a child-only product definition.

07 / Open work as of 10 November

What still needed validation.

The archive distinguishes current work from ideas that were still exploratory. These are not presented as completed features.

CAMERA

Test the interactions

Continue exploring sleep, gaze, hand gestures, motion cues, forgotten objects and seat-belt detection. Validate multi-person combinations and fallback behaviour.

MEDIA

Resolve seat-by-seat sharing

Clarify how audio and video can be routed to another seat without requiring the receiving passenger to have a separate streaming account.

ACCESSIBILITY

Do not rely on colour alone

Define additional cues for people with colour-vision differences: position, name, shape, motion and readable text.

NARRATIVE & APP

Build and test the main flows

Refine the Framer-style app into a small set of core screens; align vehicle screens with the Hyundai grid and Seon line motif; conduct user testing.

MOTION COMFORT

Keep claims evidence-led

Motion-sickness cues were discussed in the script, but the archive notes they were not yet represented in the slide deck and still needed visual-cue evidence.

SHOWCASE PLAN

Prototype → video → documentation

The earlier plan listed user testing and design freeze around 16 November, final prototype around 23 November, video and documentation around 30 November, and final showcase on 7 December.
