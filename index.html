01 / ONE SYSTEM · MANY PERSONAL ZONES

One system.
Many personal zones.

Cars are shared by many, but personalisation is usually designed around one person. MovI connects each passenger with the seat they occupy — then carries their experience across the cabin.

04 / PERSONAL ZONES

One cabin.
Different experiences.

Each passenger gets a consistent, recognisable zone. The system responds locally instead of forcing everyone into one shared setting.

01 / CONFIRM

Ambient light

Four indirect zones use a low-intensity glow to confirm recognition and communicate a passenger state without distracting the driver.

02 / PERSONALISE

Screen

Passenger-facing screens carry personal content and preferences while keeping the shared interface calm and readable.

03 / LOCALISE

Sound

Audio can stay within a passenger’s own zone. If someone is sleeping, only the affected side should be considered.

04 / COMFORT

Comfort

Local seat and temperature adjustments support individual comfort without changing the whole cabin.

05 / AWARENESS

Cabin Map

A compact driver-facing overview shows who sits where and relevant states such as sleeping or Guest mode.

06 / CONTINUITY

Personal profile

One person keeps one colour and consistent preferences across journeys, regardless of which seat they occupy.

07 / GUEST

Guest mode

Guests receive a neutral Champagne default. Their information is temporary and is not stored as a profile.

08 / ACCESS

Readable cues

Colour is never the only signal. Names, seat position and clear motion or screen cues support accessibility.

06 / DESIGN DECISIONS

Designed around
real cabin behaviour.

The direction was narrowed through scenario work, feedback and prototype tests — prioritising a clear, feasible passenger experience.

Camera, not fingerprint

Use the existing interior camera rather than adding a new fingerprint or NFC interaction.

Family car first

Begin with a shared family vehicle where passenger needs and preferences can differ at the same time.

Passenger-level logic

Personalisation follows the person and the occupied seat zone, not a fixed seat owner.

2D Cabin Map first

Prioritise a clear, compact 2D map over a more complex 3D representation.

Four indirect light zones

Keep light local and away from the driver's field of view.

One colour per person

Use Champagne as a neutral default plus eight passenger colours, supported by non-colour cues.

Safety over preference

Personal settings never override safety. The system should warn before limiting an affected zone.

Privacy by default

Consent for enrolment, local processing as the intended direction, and no saved Guest data.

Keep the interface calm

Use restrained feedback and avoid unsolicited assistant interruptions or unnecessary menus.

07 / PRIVACY · SAFETY · ACCESSIBILITY

Personal, not invasive.

A passenger-aware cabin should make people feel recognised — not watched.

Consent

Face enrolment is opt-in. Passengers can manage or delete their profile.

Guest privacy

Guest mode uses a temporary neutral state; guest information is not saved.

Local-first intent

In-car processing is the intended direction, with no unnecessary cloud profile transfer.

Accessible feedback

Names, position and interface cues complement colour; colour alone never carries meaning.
